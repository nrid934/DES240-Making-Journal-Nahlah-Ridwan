## Project Statement
I am exploring the issue of overpopulating cats, specifically in Auckland. As more felines populate outdoors, the numbers of stray and feral cats increase too. With more cats, comes the need to care for more. Shelters can get overwhelmed with numbers and can be unable to care for more. When an increased number of cats need to be cared for, who is there to do it?  

The data visualisation shows an outline of the map of Auckland, with each red circle to signify at least 5 cats within the area. The data was derived from Science Direct in 2012. The research explores stray cat numbers within the Auckland region and New Zealand as a whole. I had chosen to use the data specifically from Auckland. 

As this presentation is hosted in Auckland, the data shown was aimed to be more relavent and easier to grasp for the people who may observe it. It may be easier to see data from whole countries and not think much of it, but to see the data and know the different areas can provide a perspective that becomes easier to comprehend. If the areas area easier to think about, I hope this also highlights the magnitude of the issue within the region, and therefore the issue as a whole in general. 

In the future, I would like to see more people aware of the complications of this issue, whilst also taking appropriate measures where they can to mitigate it. The main course of action I would like to inspire others to take is to desex their cats. The backend of a cat in the corner shows that it still has its reproductive organs, this was chosen in an attempt for the audience to make the connection that desexing cats would likely improve the situation.
